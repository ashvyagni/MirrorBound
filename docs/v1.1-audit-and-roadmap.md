## 1. Architecture as it actually is

```
browser (src/web)                        server (apps/server/mirrorbound)
React 19 ─ landing, auth, character,     api/websocket.py → api/session.py
           naming, connection overlay      GameSession.step() @ 60 Hz  (1,277 lines)
Phaser 4.2.1 ─ HudScene draws ALL           ├── game/   deterministic sim
           in-game UI on the canvas         │   entities · combat · movement
           PlayScene draws the world        │   dungeon · world · progression
                  ▲                         └── agent/  observes via EventBus only
                  │ SNAPSHOT 20 Hz              telemetry → features → player_model
                  └──── INPUT/COMMAND ───►      prediction · spatial · twin/
```

The `AGENTS.md` dependency direction holds in the code: `game/` imports nothing from
`agent/`, `api/` or FastAPI, and the agent reaches the simulation only by returning a
`TwinIntent` that `game/twin_executor.py` validates. Determinism is real — every random
decision goes through `DeterministicRNG.spawn(label)` sub-streams, and
`tests/integration/test_determinism.py` plus `test_session.py` prove seed-in/state-out.

**The UI split matters for v1.1 §24.** Every in-game screen is Phaser, assembled from the
hand-drawn atlases and registered as a field on `HudScene` (`MapScreen`, `SkillScreen`,
`InventoryScreen`, `DialogueScreen`, `SettingsScreen`, `PauseScreen`, `SandboxScreen`,
`AgentScreen`, `Notifications`, `InteractPrompt`, `Toast`). React holds only the landing
page, auth, character sheet, naming and the connection notice. New v1.1 screens (quest
log, codex, world map, merchant) therefore belong in `src/web/src/game/hud/` as `Panel`
subclasses — putting them in React would visibly break the established chrome.

### 1.1 The one constraint that shapes the whole expansion

**The simulation holds exactly one room at a time.** This is the central finding.

- `GameState.room` is a single `Room`. There is no collection of live rooms.
- `MovementSystem` clamps every entity to `room.interior`; `Room.clamp` and
  `Room.is_blocked` are the only spatial authority.
- Enemies are spawned by `spawn_enemies_for_room` on first visit and thrown away on exit
  (`_enter_room` sets `state.enemies = []`).
- The full tile grid, decor list, doors and portals travel in the snapshot only when the
  room changes (`roomFull`); the client caches and rebuilds its whole floor texture then.
- There are no world-space coordinates. Position is room-local, and room ids
  (`{area_id}_room_{index}`) are what heatmaps and telemetry key on.

So "one continuous open world" cannot mean streaming chunks without rewriting movement,
collision, the snapshot contract, the renderer and every determinism test. §36 and §39 of
the brief say to prefer the lightweight system that delivers the experience, and to design
the closest high-quality alternative when the architecture makes something
disproportionate. That alternative is set out in §7.1.

### 1.2 Current world structure

| Concept | Where | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Area | `game/world/campaign.py` `AreaDef` | 6 total: 5 campaign + `the_proving` sandbox |
| Run state | `CampaignState` | `current_area`, `completed_areas`, `discovered_areas`, `looted_rooms`, `flags: set[str]`, `seals: list[str]` |
| Village | `game/world/village.py` | **one** `Room`, 1600×1120, NPCs from `VILLAGE_NPCS`, portals along the top edge, props scattered by seeded rejection sampling |
| Dungeon | `game/dungeon/generation.py` | linear `list[Room]`; doors link `north`→`index+1`, `south`→`index−1`. **No branching exists** |
| Room | `game/dungeon/room.py` | tile grid (6 types), `decor`, `doors`, `portals`, `npcs`, `enemy_spawns`, `treasure` |
| Travel | `session.py::_travel` | portals only, and only from a village or the sandbox |

Areas today: Hollow Reach (village) → Wakewood Crypt (5 rooms, tutorial) → Emberfall
(village) → Ashen Deep (6 rooms, ×1.35, Warden) → Mirror Sanctum (2 rooms, ×1.5, Mirror).
`VILLAGE_ROADS` maps each village to the portals it offers; `HOME_VILLAGE` maps each
dungeon back. The brief's diagnosis is exactly right: portals *are* the connective tissue,
and the map screen is how you move around the campaign.

## 2. Content inventory — the real numbers

The brief's estimates are off in two places, so these are the counts to design against.

**Weapons — 4, not 8.** `game/combat/weapons.py` defines `iron_sword`, `hunter_bow`,
`ember_staff`, `frost_staff`, plus `bare_hands` (the unarmed fallback) and `admin_stick`
(a testing tool, excluded from shops/drops by `ADMIN_WEAPONS`). The eight *sprites* are
misleading: `swordA/B/C` are the three combo frames of one sword, and the shield sheets
belong to `ShieldOverlay` drawing the Aegis ability, not to a shield weapon. Two weapons
are carried at once; `Q` swaps them.

**Abilities — 12 defined, 10 player-facing.** Bound to weapons, not to the player:
`WeaponDef.abilities` grants 2–3 each, and `Inventory.ability_slots` derives the four keys
from the carried pair. `mirror_nova` and `mirror_volley` are the boss's own. Empty hands
grant only `shadow_dash`. **This is a genuinely good system and must survive** — it makes
"what you carry" the real build choice, so new weapons are the natural way to add
abilities.

**Skills — 12 nodes, and the brief's complaint is fair.** Four branches
(MOBILITY/COMBAT/MAGIC/SURVIVAL) × three tiers, each requiring the tier below. But every
node resolves into one of 13 numeric fields on `StatModifiers`, so all twelve are
percentages — there is no node that changes how the game plays. Expanding this means
teaching `StatModifiers` (or a successor) to carry behaviour, not just adding rows.

**Enemies — 13 archetypes**, each with a stated design question: `skeleton`, `archer`,
`hound`, `slime`, `acolyte`, `brute`, `scarab`, `spitter`, `sprout`, `shardling`, `dummy`,
plus `warden` (guardian) and `mirror` (boss). `elite_of()` derives elites; `armed_with()`
re-derives a def so the Mirror fights with the player's weapon.

**Room templates — 11**, across `RoomType` ENTRANCE / COMBAT (×6) / EXPLORATION /
TREASURE / ELITE / GUARDIAN / BOSS. `RoomType.EVENT` exists but maps to the exploration
template; `RoomType.VILLAGE` is unused by the generator.

**Economy — gold only, with no sink for anything else.** Two vendors (Oren: 4 weapons +
`wolf_fang`; Siv: 2 potions + `ember_heart`), `LootTable.gold_min/max` per enemy, and a
lump on dungeon completion. `essence`, `shards` and `relics` are counted in
`Inventory.resources` and **spend nowhere** — `RESOURCES` even documents essence as
fuelling "relic crafting later". That is the economy hook §18 asks for, already half-built.

**NPCs — 4 definitions across 2 villages.** Emberfall reuses the *same* `NpcDef` objects
as Hollow Reach, so the smith and apothecary say identical lines in both. Dialogue is
`dict[str, tuple[str, ...]]` keyed by at most four quest states (`intro`, `quest_active`,
`twin_rescued`, `area_cleared`), resolved by `dialogue_for()` with `{player}`/`{twin}`
substitution. NPCs are placed as blocking decor under their own sprite name and never move.

**Quests — no quest system.** `CampaignState.flags` is an untyped `set[str]` and `seals` a
`list[str]`. The elder setting `quest_active` is the whole of it. §5's side quests need a
real object.

**Difficulty — one multiplier.** `AreaDef.difficulty` (1.0 / 1.35 / 1.5) scales enemy
health ×d and damage ×(1+(d−1)×0.7) in `scaled_for_region`. Speed, range and wind-up are
deliberately left alone, which is the right instinct and should be preserved.

**Biomes — 3** (`grove`, `ruins`, `crypt`) plus `sandbox`, each with a palette in
`constants.ts BIOMES` and one drawn tile atlas. There is no mountain, forest, road or cave
biome.

**Audio** is procedural WebAudio synthesis in `AudioManager.ts` — no recorded tracks, and
ambience is selected per biome (`BIOMES[x].ambient`), so per-region audio is a small
extension rather than new infrastructure.

**Save** is versioned JSON in per-profile slot directories (`auto` + `slot-1..12`),
written at villages, hearths and the twin rescue. The learned player model *is* persisted
per slot in an opaque `agent` block.

## 3. Asset inventory for reuse (§35)

595 PNGs, 146 atlas JSON/PNG pairs, all sliced by `src/web/scripts/build_atlas.py` into
generated TypeScript modules. `propArt.ts` maps a server decor `kind` to a frame plus a
**world-unit height** — that height is the field that keeps a crate from drawing bigger
than a house, and any new prop needs one.

What exists, by sheet (8 frames each unless noted): `flora` (tree×3, treeBig×2, bush×3),
`stone` (rock×3, rockBig×2, rubble×3), `ruins` (pillar×2, brokenPillar×2, statue, well,
crate×2), `ruinsProps` (archBroken, columnFallen, stoneBench, urnCracked, stairFragment,
runeStone, sarcophagus, banneredRubble), `cryptProps` (bonePile, skullStack, cobweb,
hangingChain, coffinCracked, candleCluster, ironCage, rootIntrusion), `groveProps`
(fallenTrunk, stump, berryBush, reeds, fernClump, mossRock, fencePost, cartWheel),
`groundcover` (flowers×4, grassTuft×3, log), `buildings` (hut×3, hutBig×2, forge, stall,
banner), `lights` (5), `doors` (4), `villagers` + `villagersSide` (elder, smith,
apothecary, hearth, villager×4), `chest` (8-frame opening), `tilesGrove/Ruins/Crypt`, and
12 enemy families × 4 states.

**Gaps that v1.1 must compose rather than commission:**

- No mountain, cliff, bridge or cave art. Cliffs and passes must be built from `rock_big`,
  `stone` and `T_STONE`/`T_WALL` tile massing; a mountain pass is a narrow walkable
  corridor between impassable rock, which the existing collision already supports.
- No road tile beyond `T_PATH`, which is exactly what roads are painted from today.
- **Only 4 generic villager bodies.** §3 and §4 want farmers, guards, children and
  workers. The honest route is recombination — variant index, `scale`, and tint over the
  existing 4 bodies + `villagersSide` profiles — not new sheets. A "guard" is a villager
  variant standing at a gate with guard dialogue, and that reads correctly.
- `WorldRenderer` falls back to `PropPainter`'s procedural Canvas2D for any decor kind
  without a sheet, so a new prop kind renders as *something* from day one.

## 4. Agent and AI layer (preserve, do not disturb)

`agent/` observes through `EventBus` and returns intents. Nine EWMA player traits,
order-1–3 Markov action prediction with a 30-second weight half-life, seven spatial
heatmap layers, pattern detect/lost lifecycle, and a nine-dimension twin style model.
`TwinV0Controller` scores ten intents (FOLLOW, PROTECT, INTERCEPT, FLANK, ATTACK, ASSIST,
RETREAT, DISTRACT, REPOSITION, EXPLORE) with hysteresis and posture momentum.
`MirrorController` picks counters weighted by model confidence, with stance commitment
(`STANCE_SECONDS`, `STANCE_FATIGUE`, `STANCE_FLOOR`) so it visibly changes its mind.

**v1.1 risk:** the spatial heatmaps key on a shared coordinate space across rooms
(`FEATURE_STATUS.md` already flags this). A much larger world makes that worse — hot
cells from a 4096-wide region would blend with a 960-wide dungeon room. Region-aware
spatial keying should be folded into the world work, not left to the AI phase.

## 6. Defects and technical debt found

### 6.1 Sixteen failing tests, one cause — and a live player-facing regression

Commit `9402d47` ("implement postgres database and jwt authentication") wrapped four
commands in `session.py::_apply_commands` with `if not self.is_admin: reject`. Three are
legitimately debug-only (`SPAWN`, `GIVE`, `CONFIGURE_BOSS`). The fourth is **`RESPEC`** —
which `GAMEPLAY.md` documents as a player feature ("Unlearn all", on the skill screen, in
a village, out of combat) and `_respec()` still enforces those rules itself.

So on `main` today, no ordinary player can unlearn their skill tree. That is a real
regression, not merely a test-fixture problem, and the 16 failures are the tests correctly
reporting it. Fixing this is the first thing I will do, because the v1.1 skill-tree
expansion makes respec *more* important, not less.

### 6.2 Bumping `SAVE_VERSION` silently deletes every save

`save.py::read_save` rejects any file whose `version != SAVE_VERSION` and returns `None`,
which the session reads as "no save" and starts a fresh campaign. There is no migration
path at all. The brief (§30, §31) requires the expanded state not to corrupt existing
saves, so a real `migrate(data) -> data` step keyed on the stored version has to land
*before* any v1.1 field is added to the save shape.

### 6.3 The JWT signing secret is committed to the repository

`apps/.auth_secret` is **tracked in git** (`git ls-files` confirms it), and
`api/auth.py::get_secret()` falls back to reading it when `JWT_SECRET` is unset. Since
this repository is pushed to GitHub, that file is the key for forging auth tokens for any
deployment relying on the fallback. `.gitignore` covers `.env*` but not this file. It
should be untracked, ignored, and rotated. Flagging rather than acting: removing a secret
from history is the user's call, and it is outside the v1.1 gameplay scope.

### 6.4 `api/session.py` holds gameplay rules

At 1,277 lines it owns area transitions, cutscene beats, room logic, shops, saves and
respec, which `AGENTS.md` says belongs in `game/`. `FEATURE_STATUS.md` already
acknowledges this. v1.1 adds a great deal of world logic, so **new world behaviour goes
into `game/world/` modules that `session.py` calls**, rather than growing this file.

### 6.5 Stale documentation (corrected as each area is touched)

- `GAMEPLAY.md` describes six freely-assignable abilities with four equipped; abilities
  have since become weapon-bound, and the staves now have three each.
- `GAMEPLAY.md` says the learned player model is deliberately not saved; it is now
  persisted per slot in the save's `agent` block.
- `hud/MapScreen.ts`'s doc comment and `FEATURE_STATUS.md` both claim map travel
  auto-completes skipped prerequisites and grants their rewards. `session.py::_leave_area`
  now correctly refuses gated areas — the comments describe behaviour that was removed.
- `FEATURE_STATUS.md` says `spitter`, `sprout` and `shardling` "have art but no server
  archetypes". All three have full archetypes and dedicated room templates.

### 6.6 Village decoration will not scale to an authored village

`village.py::_decorate` places props by seeded rejection sampling with a road check. It
produces a plausible field, but §3 requires a *legible* village — blacksmith beside the
forge, farmer by farmland, guard at the entrance. That needs authored layout data per
village, with scatter reduced to filling the gaps between authored anchors.

## 7. Implementation plan

### 7.1 The overworld decision (§1, §2) — regions as rooms

**Rejected:** true chunk-streaming with world-space coordinates. It would require
rewriting `MovementSystem`, `Room` collision, the snapshot contract, `WorldRenderer`'s
one-texture-per-room floor composition, and every determinism test — for an experience the
alternative delivers.

**Chosen:** treat the overworld as a graph of large authored **wilderness regions**, each
one an ordinary `Room` exactly as a village already is, connected by **edge transitions**
instead of portals. Walking off the north edge of the Ember Road puts you at the south
edge of the Kiln Terraces, with no menu, no portal glyph and no travel confirmation.

Why this is the right call:

- It reuses `Room`, `MovementSystem`, `CollisionSystem`, `WorldRenderer`, `PropPainter`,
  decor collision and the tile pipeline **without modification**.
- Villages already prove the pattern: a large, safe, NPC-populated, portal-bearing `Room`.
- The brief's own target sequence — village → roads → grasslands → landmarks → mountain
  pass → village — is a *sequence of places you walk through*, which is what a chain of
  regions joined at their edges is.
- Region size is bounded by snapshot cost, and the cost is acceptable: at `TILE = 32`, a
  4096×3072 region is 128×96 = 12,288 tile ints ≈ 40 KB of JSON, sent **once** on
  transition (the same moment the client already rebuilds its floor). That is ~7× today's
  1600×1120 village and well within budget. 4096×3072 is the cap I will design to.
- Determinism is unaffected: each region gets `rng.spawn(f"area:{id}")` exactly as areas
  do now.

What has to be built for it:

1. `Door` gains `east`/`west` linking (the model already has the sides; `generation.py`
   only ever wires north/south). Edge transitions reuse `Door` with a new `kind="edge"`
   and an area target, or a `Portal` subtype with `kind="edge"` that the client draws as
   nothing.
2. `AreaDef` gains neighbour links (`north`/`south`/`east`/`west` → area id) so the region
   graph is authored data, and `VILLAGE_ROADS` becomes one case of it.
3. `session.py::_travel` keeps refusing portal travel from dungeons, but edge transitions
   are allowed from any overworld region — that is what makes the world continuous.
4. Region-aware spatial keys for the heatmaps (§4 risk).

Portals survive where the lore earns them (§26): the Sanctum's descent, and unlocked
fast travel between discovered settlements.

### 7.2 Phase order

The brief's ten phases, sequenced against what actually blocks what. Each phase ends with
tests passing and a local commit.

**Phase 0 — Foundations.** Fix §6.1 (un-gate `RESPEC`, restore 16 tests to green). Land
§6.2 (save migration framework) before any save field changes. Correct the §6.5 stale
docs. Baseline a performance measurement so §29 has a before.

**Phase 1 — World foundation (§2, §22).** Region graph in `campaign.py`; edge transitions;
`game/world/overworld.py` building wilderness regions; mountain/forest massing from
existing art; roads connecting Hollow Reach → Emberfall on foot. Goal: the world is
walkable end to end before anything is put in it.

**Phase 2 — Villages and NPCs (§3, §4, §14, §19).** Authored village layouts; villager
routines; per-village merchant identity; dialogue categories.

**Phase 3 — Quests and lore (§5, §6, §13, §15, §27).** `game/world/quest.py`; codex;
travellers and roadside storytelling; region lore.

**Phase 4 — Dungeons (§8–§11).** Three archetypes with distinct loops; branching via
east/west doors; keys, locked doors, secrets.

**Phase 5 — Progression (§16, §17, §18, §23).** Behavioural skill tree; weapon archetypes
and upgrades; spend `essence`/`shards`.

**Phase 6 — Bosses and story (§12).** Two or three regional bosses from existing enemy
art, each with a region, a telegraph vocabulary and phases. The Twin → Mirror confrontation
is untouched.

**Phase 7 — Difficulty and balance (§20, §21).** Enemy behaviour and encounter
composition, measured with the existing combat probe rather than guessed.

**Phase 8 — UI (§24, §25, §26).** Quest log, codex, expanded map with fast travel, merchant
and upgrade screens — all as `Panel` subclasses in `hud/`.

**Phase 9 — Audio, performance, polish (§28, §29, §33).** Region ambience; profile and
optimise only what measurement flags; full-playthrough testing.

### 7.3 Non-negotiables carried through every phase

- Python owns truth; the browser renders snapshots. No outcome decided client-side.
- No `random.*` in `game/` or `agent/`; every stream is a named `DeterministicRNG.spawn`.
- No `hash()` on strings for anything persisted.
- `game/` never imports `agent/`, `api/` or FastAPI; combat publishes events instead.
- New world logic lands in `game/world/`, not in `api/session.py` (§6.4).
- Determinism tests stay green; anything touching `game/core` runs the full suite.
- The Twin → Mirror ending, the weapon-bound ability system, the existing UI chrome and
  the five original areas are preserved, not replaced.
