# v1.2 — mining, attributes, stones, a living wild, and a twin that looks after itself

Written alongside the implementation, 2026-09-27/28. This is the reasoning, not the reference:
what the expansion does is in [GAMEPLAY.md](../GAMEPLAY.md), what is built and what is not is in
[FEATURE_STATUS.md](../FEATURE_STATUS.md).

## The brief, and the one decision that made it a system

Five asks came in together:

1. Mine resources in dungeons — iron, gold, diamond, adamantine, mithril and more — each giving
   its own buff when built into a weapon, and on the rare side.
2. Relic stones, rarely dropped, fused into weapons, at 1/1000 or 1/10000 by stone level.
3. More stats than vitality/combat/mana, so that spending points and levelling is harder and
   more interesting, **and the skill tree should be based on them**.
4. A twin that understands the player's playstyle *and the game*: if it learns it can drop to
   low health, it should buy itself health potions and use them like a player would.
5. A larger living overworld: passive animals to hunt and eat, hostile wild animals, grassfield
   and tundra biomes, and a hunger bar that reduces combat and defence when it empties.

Read as five features this is five systems bolted to the same game. The load-bearing decision
was to read them as **one economy**, and the sentence that did it came back from the owner in
four words: *materials should feed you attributes*.

```
  fighting  →  XP   →  levels  →  skill points  →  the tree        (behaviour)
  mining    →  ore  →  training  →  attributes  ──┘ gates tiers 3-4   (numbers)
```

One attribute per skill branch; one ore per attribute. Ore is therefore not a fourth currency
looking for a shop — it is the only way to open the bottom half of a tree the player already
wanted. And the gate runs the other way too: 20 levels pay 19 attribute points, while tier 3
alone asks 25 across the five branches, so **the tree cannot be finished by levelling**.
`test_materials.py` asserts that inequality directly, because it is the single number holding
the two layers together and a later balance pass could break it by accident.

| Attribute | Branch | Ore |
|---|---|---|
| Vigour | Survival | iron |
| Might | Combat | adamantine |
| Finesse | Mobility | mithril |
| Focus | Magic | gold |
| Bond | Mirror | silver |

## Two places the literal request was arithmetic that does not work

Both were kept as *intent* and changed as *mechanism*, with the numbers shown rather than a
refusal.

**"+0.3% damage per iron."** On the real weapon table that is 0.042 damage per iron — invisible,
and a quantity you add until you run out of iron rather than a decision. Worse, it makes every
material the same material with a different multiplier. So materials change a weapon's **shape**
instead: iron makes it heavier (+12% damage, +8% cooldown), mithril makes it quick (−22%
cooldown, −35% knockback), obsidian splits armour and chips (+33% pierce, −6% damage). Every one
is a trade, so "what is this weapon for" is a question with an answer.

**Affinity** is the half that makes a material have an identity rather than a stat line: gold in
a staff is gold doing what gold does, and gold on a sword edge halves the upside and doubles the
penalty. The smith says which before the ore is spent.

**"1/10000 for a tier-3 stone."** At the campaign's kill count that is roughly fifty
playthroughs for one stone, which is not rarity, it is absence. The requested rates were kept
exactly — 1/200, 1/1000, 1/10000 — and a **pity floor** put underneath them: a drought counter
per tier, saved with the run so it cannot be farmed by reconnecting, guaranteeing a tier-1 by 80
kills, a tier-2 by 400 and a tier-3 by 2000. The floor is what makes the rate *sayable*:
"vanishingly rare, and never worse than one in two thousand" is a sentence a test can check,
where "it feels rare" is not. Each regional boss also awards a tier-3 outright, so the best
stones have a route that is a fight rather than a dice roll. The Mirror awards none: the ending
is not a loot table.

## Protecting what v1.1 measured

v1.1 measured the regional bosses at 0.67 (Warden) and 0.89 (Glasswork) clear after fixing their
telegraphs. Two v1.2 features could have moved those numbers invisibly, and both were built so
they cannot.

**Armour.** Obsidian's pierce needs something to pierce, so enemies gained an `armour` field.
Three archetypes were given armour with **exactly reciprocal health cuts** — SLIME 115→92 at
0.20, BRUTE 180→135 at 0.25, SHARDLING 140→105 at 0.25 — so effective health against a weapon
with no obsidian in it is unchanged to the decimal, and every difficulty figure v1.1 published
still describes the fight it was measured on.

**Hunger freezes at a boss door.** A −20% damage, +15% damage-taken debuff multiplies straight
into a clear rate with nothing in the measurement able to see it, and a player who walked in
hungry would be fighting a different fight from the one that was tuned with no way to tell. So
the drain stops while a boss is armed and the penalty lifts with it.

A third protection is in the design rather than the numbers: **food does not heal.** Food that
heals is a cheap potion, and the moment it is a cheap potion the apothecary has nothing to sell
and the gold economy the v1.1 bench was priced against goes with it. This was got wrong first
time — "Fed" was originally out-of-combat regeneration — and six existing tests caught it by
asserting exact health values, which is the best argument available for asserting exact values.

## Hunger: on activity, not on a clock

Three points per thousand units walked and 0.22 per action. A hunger bar on
wall-clock time punishes reading the journal, comparing two weapons in a menu, or standing up to
answer the door — none of which is what the mechanic is about. On activity it is a cost of
expedition.

Three bands rather than one falling number, because a bar you can only lose by is a chore and a
bar you can win by is a reason to cook: Fed (+6% damage, +5% pace), Fine (nothing), Hungry (−20%
damage, +15% taken, −8% pace). Hungry is **flat, not a slope**: a penalty that keeps growing
turns a mistake into a spiral, and there is no way out of a spiral when the food is three regions
behind you. Nothing here can kill.

## The twin: three defects, not a missing feature

The headline ask was a twin that "should buy itself health potions and use them accordingly just
as a player". Auditing what stood in the way found the feature was not missing so much as
**blocked**:

- `loot.py`'s `_apply` routed every consumable to `state.player.inventory` whoever walked over
  it. A companion with an inventory could not own a potion at all.
- `HEAL` was declared in `INTENT_TYPES`, produced by nothing, and filed in `twin_executor.py`
  under the *movement* intents. On the one occasion it might have appeared, the twin would have
  walked.
- It had no money.

So it did not need a bigger brain. It needed a purse, a stock nothing steals, and a `HEAL` that
drinks. (`COMBO` remains declared and unproduced — left alone deliberately: adding a fifth
offensive intent that duplicates ATTACK's job is not what the brief asked for.)

**Everything is scored in the same pass.** HEAL, EAT and SHOP are candidates alongside ATTACK and
RETREAT rather than special cases handled ahead of them, and that is what makes the result read
as judgement rather than script. The clearest example: a drink is discounted by how close the
nearest enemy is, and RETREAT is rewarded by exactly the same thing. Neither knows the other
exists. "Back off, then drink" is the two curves crossing.

**It is held to the player's own numbers** — same drink duration, same six-second shared
cooldown, same refusal at full health, same interruption on being hit with nothing consumed. A
companion that could chain potions six times faster than the player would be a different
creature, and the interesting version of "it plays like a player" is the one where it is bound by
the player's rules.

**Three new style dimensions**, each fed by both learning channels:

| Dimension | Imitation (from the player) | Experience (from itself) |
|---|---|---|
| `drink_threshold` | The health fraction the player drinks at — `ITEM_USED` now carries `atHealth`, captured *before* the heal, or it would only ever say "nearly full" | A HEAL that cost it damage pushes it to drink earlier |
| `stock_target` | Watching the player buy potions | Going down with an empty pack |
| `thrift` | Watching the player shop | Dying broke |

`TWIN_DOWNED` carries `potions`, because going down teaches **opposite** lessons depending on
what was in the pack: died holding one is a timing mistake, died with nothing is a supply
mistake. One event, two conclusions, and the count is what tells them apart.

The twin's purse is a quarter of the gold it *personally* picks up — rounded rather than
truncated, since enemies drop 1–8 gold and truncation would have made the feature arrive an hour
into a session. It is the player's purse paying, and what it buys back is a companion that stops
going down.

## Reuse before new assets (§35), and not overengineering it (§36)

Most of the expansion's content cost is art that did not have to be drawn:

- The five wild animals are all the Gloom Hound's sheet at different scales and tints.
- Ore veins are the existing `rockBig` decor frames, tinted per material.
- Biome floors are procedural: a new biome is about sixteen hex values in the client's `BIOMES`
  table, not a tileset — which is why adding a grassfield and a tundra was affordable.
- Cooking happens at the hearth that already existed, and attribute training at the smith,
  apothecary and elder who already stood in every village. A dedicated trainer would have meant a
  new role, a new portrait and a new stall in every settlement plan.
- Somebody selling food is Bram, Esk and Brek — three NPCs already in the world — rather than a
  fourth merchant archetype.

The One-Room constraint made the two new regions nearly free: `GameState` holds exactly one
`Room`, regions *are* Rooms, so more regions costs authored content and nothing structural.

## Things worth knowing that are not features

**The HUD fixture is generated, not maintained.** `src/web/tests/fixtures/village.json` is a
recorded snapshot the frontend tests drive themselves from — the right shape of test, because it
exercises the interface against something the server actually produced. The cost is that it goes
stale *silently*: the client typecheck does not reach test fixtures, so it kept passing while
describing a game with no hunger bar and no ore. `tools/fixtures/regen_village.py` regenerates it
from a live session at a fixed seed, so a regeneration that changes anything is a contract change
worth reading in the diff.

**The agent debug screen shows the five dimensions it is most confident about**, not the first
five declared. With twelve dimensions and room for five, insertion order meant the three new ones
could never appear on the one screen whose job is to explain what the twin has learned.

**This machine's timing variance is ±2.4×.** Mid-expansion the suite appeared to triple in
runtime (34s → 105s). A cProfile pass found nothing (the suspected function was 1.8% of total),
and a proper A/B — three commits, three runs each, stashing between — showed the same commit
swinging 2.4× on identical code. There was no regression. See
[perf-baseline.md](perf-baseline.md); v1.2 timings are only meaningful against a baseline taken
in the same session.

## Still open

- **`apps/.auth_secret` is tracked in git.** Flagged in the v1.1 audit and still true. It needs
  untracking, a history rewrite and rotation, which is the owner's call and not a gameplay change.
- **`api/session.py` still holds gameplay rules** that AGENTS.md places in `game/`. v1.2 added its
  world logic to `game/world/` and `game/progression/` rather than growing the file, and moved the
  twin's purchase path into `twin_executor.py` rather than the session — but it did not shrink it.
- **The new layer is unmeasured against a human.** The scripted probe does not mine, train, fit
  or socket, so every difficulty figure in [playtest-results.md](playtest-results.md) describes a
  player with none of v1.2's numbers. That is the honest floor, not the expected experience.
